# Project Concept

## 1. Core Concept

A service for culturally accurate creative generation based on verified heritage of the peoples of Russia.

A user describes a practical or creative task — for example, to develop a gift idea, visual concept, event concept, text, story, naming direction, or other creative material. The system generates a contemporary result using a curated body of verified cultural sources rather than relying only on the model's general knowledge or uncontrolled internet data.

Where relevant, generated results can be accompanied by:
- an explanation of which cultural elements were used;
- references to the source material;
- notes on the historical, regional, ethnic, or ritual context of those elements.

The project is not primarily an educational encyclopedia. Its main purpose is to make verified cultural knowledge usable in contemporary creative and everyday tasks.

---

## 2. Problem

There are two connected problems.

### 2.1. Cultural heritage is preserved, but often poorly integrated into current practice

A significant amount of information about regional traditions, folklore, crafts, rituals, symbols, local history, language, music, clothing, cuisine, and other forms of cultural heritage already exists in archives, museums, academic publications, cultural institutions, and digital collections.

However, the existence of this information does not automatically lead to its active use. Much of it remains separated from the everyday and professional contexts in which people create events, visual materials, gifts, media, brands, stories, or other cultural products.

The project treats preservation as more than storage. A tradition is more likely to remain socially relevant when it continues to be interpreted and used in new contexts.

### 2.2. Existing generative AI is useful for creative tasks, but is unreliable in culturally specific contexts

General-purpose generative models can already create texts, visual concepts, event ideas, stories, names, and other creative outputs. However, when asked to work with a specific regional or ethnic culture, they may:
- reproduce stereotypes;
- mix different regions, peoples, or historical periods;
- invent traditions or symbols;
- use culturally irrelevant visual clichés;
- provide no clear source for a cultural claim.

For generic creative work this may be acceptable. For projects explicitly based on a real culture, it becomes a quality problem.

The project therefore focuses on a narrower task: **creative generation where cultural accuracy and traceability matter**.

---

## 3. User Need

The user does not necessarily have an initial goal to study cultural heritage.

The user already has a practical task:
- create a gift;
- develop a visual or communication concept;
- prepare an event;
- write a story, greeting, script, or campaign;
- use regional identity in a project;
- create something connected to a place, community, or tradition.

The existing difficulty is finding cultural references that are both:
1. relevant to the task;
2. sufficiently trustworthy to use.

The service reduces the gap between **cultural knowledge** and **creative application**.

---

## 4. Target Audiences

The project should distinguish between the customer, the end user, and the cultural data provider.

### Primary target audience: professionals who regularly create culturally contextualized materials

Examples:
- designers;
- creative and advertising teams;
- event producers;
- cultural project teams;
- regional brands;
- museum and festival teams;
- PR and communications specialists;
- protocol and corporate gift teams;
- creators working with local identity.

Their need is recurrent rather than occasional: they repeatedly need concepts, references, themes, stories, symbols, gifts, event ideas, and other outputs connected to a region or cultural context.

For the project, this audience is more important than a purely mass consumer audience because it has a clearer repeated use case.

### Secondary target audience: individual users with occasional creative tasks

Examples:
- a person choosing a culturally meaningful gift;
- someone preparing a family or community celebration;
- a traveller looking for a culturally grounded activity;
- a person creating a story, greeting, or personal project connected to a region.

This audience is useful for broad adoption and public engagement, but the frequency of use is likely to be lower.

### Institutional stakeholders

Potential stakeholders include:
- cultural institutions;
- museums and archives;
- universities and researchers;
- regional cultural organizations;
- government-supported cultural programs;
- communities and recognized experts associated with specific traditions.

Their role is different from the role of the end user. They may provide, validate, structure, or maintain cultural knowledge and benefit from wider, more accurate reuse of heritage.

---

## 5. Public and State Value

The project directly addresses the competition theme: the use of AI and digital technologies for the preservation, popularization, and reinterpretation of national cultures.

Its public value is not based on replacing archives or educational resources. It is based on increasing the practical circulation of verified cultural knowledge.

The project can contribute to:
- preservation through continued use and reinterpretation;
- popularization of regional and ethnic heritage;
- intergenerational transmission of cultural knowledge;
- reduction of stereotypical or fabricated representations of cultures in AI-generated content;
- stronger use of cultural heritage in creative industries;
- improved access to reliable cultural references for non-experts.

For the state or a cultural institution, the project is therefore not only a content service. It is a mechanism for moving cultural heritage from passive storage into contemporary cultural production.

---

## 6. Core Principles

### Cultural accuracy over generic stylistic imitation

The project should not reduce cultures to a generic “ethnic style”. Cultural references must be tied, where possible, to a specific people, locality, time period, practice, or source.

### Verified sources

The knowledge base should rely on identifiable and credible sources such as:
- museum and archive collections;
- academic and ethnographic publications;
- official cultural heritage resources;
- materials from recognized cultural institutions;
- expert-reviewed or community-validated materials.

The project should not claim that all generated content is automatically “true”. It should distinguish between:
- a source-supported cultural fact;
- an interpretation based on cultural references;
- a newly generated creative result.

### Traceability

A key differentiator is the ability to explain where the cultural basis of a result came from.

Traceability is important both for user trust and for competition positioning: the value is not just generation, but generation based on controlled cultural evidence.

### Contemporary use, not reconstruction for its own sake

The goal is not to reproduce historical life exactly unless a task explicitly requires it.

The service uses heritage as source material for contemporary creative work while preserving the context of the original reference.

### Respect for sensitive cultural material

Not every element of cultural heritage should be freely transformed into commercial or entertainment content.

Religious, ritual, sacred, restricted, or community-sensitive practices may require additional restrictions, expert review, or exclusion from generation.

---

## 7. What the Project Is Not

The project is **not**:
- a general encyclopedia of the peoples of Russia;
- a replacement for museums, archives, or academic databases;
- primarily a tourism map or route planner;
- a generic AI assistant with an “ethnic style” prompt;
- a platform whose main purpose is to teach users cultural history;
- a system that treats all online cultural information as equally reliable;
- a project that defines culture through genetics or ancestry.

Educational value, tourism scenarios, and deeper exploration of sources can exist, but they are secondary to the central use case: **applying verified cultural knowledge to a concrete creative task**.

---

## 8. Competitive Position

The project sits between two existing categories.

### Cultural databases and heritage platforms

Strengths:
- reliable information;
- institutional or academic sourcing.

Weakness:
- the user usually has to search, interpret, and transform information independently before it becomes useful in a creative task.

### General-purpose generative AI

Strengths:
- convenient;
- fast;
- capable of producing ready-to-use creative outputs.

Weakness:
- cultural claims may be unverifiable, stereotypical, mixed, or fabricated.

### Project position

The project combines:
- the reliability and provenance of a curated cultural knowledge base;
- the practical utility of generative AI.

Its main differentiation is therefore:

**verified cultural context + creative generation + source traceability.**

The project should not claim that no analogues exist. The stronger claim is that existing solutions usually specialize either in cultural information or in general creative generation, while this project is focused specifically on culturally grounded creative work.

---

## 9. MVP Business Hypothesis

The MVP should test the following hypothesis:

> Professionals and individual users who need culturally specific creative material value an AI system more when it can produce a usable result based on verified cultural sources and show the basis of that result.

The MVP does not need to cover all cultures, regions, or creative categories.

For competition purposes, a narrow but convincing domain is preferable to broad nominal coverage.

The MVP should be sufficient to demonstrate:
- a real and clearly defined user problem;
- access to a credible cultural source base;
- a meaningful difference from a generic LLM response;
- several realistic user scenarios;
- traceability of cultural references;
- a path to expansion into additional regions, cultures, and creative tasks.

---

## 10. Key Assumptions to Validate

Before treating the concept as proven, the team should validate several assumptions.

1. **Demand:** target professionals actually encounter tasks where culturally specific references are needed often enough to justify a dedicated tool.
2. **Quality gap:** generic AI outputs are meaningfully worse in these tasks because of factual errors, stereotypes, or lack of provenance.
3. **Trust:** source references and cultural validation materially increase user confidence.
4. **Usability of heritage data:** available sources can be structured well enough to support generation.
5. **Expert involvement:** a practical validation process can be built without making content production prohibitively expensive.
6. **Scope:** a focused set of cultures and creative tasks is enough to demonstrate value before nationwide expansion.

These assumptions should be tested through interviews, example tasks, comparison with general-purpose models, and expert review.

---

## 11. Main Risks

### Weak frequency of use

For ordinary consumers, the need may be occasional. This is why professional users with recurring creative tasks are the stronger initial segment.

### Excessive scope

“Culture of all peoples of Russia” is too broad for an MVP. Attempting full coverage would weaken both data quality and implementation credibility.

### Cultural simplification

There is a risk of turning complex traditions into visual clichés or decontextualized motifs. The project must preserve local and historical specificity.

### Data quality and validation cost

Reliable cultural data is fragmented across institutions and publications. Collecting, structuring, and validating it may become the main operational cost of the project.

### Intellectual property

A historical fact, a traditional motif, a museum image, a contemporary photograph, and a modern scholarly text have different legal conditions of use. Source provenance and usage rights must be treated separately.

### Misrepresentation

The stronger the project claims cultural accuracy, the more damaging incorrect outputs become. The project therefore needs explicit limits on what “verified” means.

---

## 12. Development Direction After MVP

If the core hypothesis is validated, development can proceed along three independent axes:

1. **Coverage:** more regions, peoples, traditions, and source collections.
2. **Creative domains:** more categories of professional and personal creative tasks.
3. **Institutional participation:** partnerships with museums, archives, universities, regional cultural organizations, and expert communities.

The long-term asset of the project is not the language model itself.

The strategic asset is the **structured, verified, reusable cultural knowledge layer and the methodology for safely converting that knowledge into contemporary creative outputs**.

---

## 13. One-Sentence Definition

**A generative AI service that helps people create contemporary culturally specific content and concepts using verified heritage sources rather than unverified model knowledge or cultural stereotypes.**
