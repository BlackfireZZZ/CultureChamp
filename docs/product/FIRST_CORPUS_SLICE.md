# First corpus slice (S01 proposal)

## Hypothesis and acceptance check

The first evaluable slice can help a cultural project team draft contemporary,
source-aware **text** about changing everyday practices and the environment among
Indigenous peoples of Primorye. This is a proposal to test with a qualified reviewer
and users, not a claim that the available PDFs are approved cultural authorities.
The slice fails if the proposed briefs cannot be answered with precise, relevant
passages after review, or if the output treats a broad regional account as a fact
about every community.

## Boundary

- **Geography and time:** Primorye in the southern Russian Far East, primarily the
  twentieth and early twenty-first centuries discussed by the candidates.
- **People:** keep the specific people named in a cited passage (for example,
  Udege or Nanai) rather than assigning a trait to all Indigenous peoples. This
  corpus is too thin for a community-specific style, language, motif, or ritual
  generation claim.
- **Candidates for review:** PDF-02 (Danilova and Karim, 2020) for an
  ethnoecological research perspective and PDF-03 (Gvozdev, 2024) for a historical
  and policy-oriented account. See the exact files and hashes in the
  [fixture inventory](../../data/retrieval-fixtures/README.md). Both are secondary
  scholarly accounts, not community endorsements. PDF-01 (Kazin, 2022) is outside
  this geographic and topic slice; retain it only as an extraction/control fixture.
- **Corpus status:** the supplied PDFs are authorized for local application and model testing. Exact-revision publication and extraction checks are tracked in the [source policy](SOURCE_POLICY.md).

## Three briefs to validate

| Brief | Intended user and usable output | Candidate evidence to check | Boundary and success condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Exhibition introduction | A Primorye museum educator needs a 150-word opening panel and three discussion prompts about how livelihoods and identity change over time. | PDF-03 discusses material culture, livelihoods, identity and policy; PDF-02 discusses adaptation and environmental context. | Attribute each historical interpretation to its author and page. Avoid speaking on behalf of a community. A reviewer can trace each claim to an approved passage. |
| 2. Public event concept | A cultural centre plans a contemporary conversation on relationships between local environments and changing ways of life. Produce a title, a short invitation, and a discussion outline. | PDF-02 frames the ethnoecological research question; PDF-03 provides a distinct historical perspective. | Do not stage or imitate ceremonies, costumes or sacred practices. Treat speakers and community participation as proposed collaboration, not existing consent. |
| 3. Editorial brief | A regional publication wants a 200-word text brief for an illustrated explainer on change in everyday material practices, plus a list of facts needing confirmation. | PDF-03 offers examples and period context; PDF-02 provides an environmental lens. | No invented Udege/Nanai vocabulary, symbols, visual patterns or universal claims. Separate source-supported facts, author interpretations and new editorial suggestions. |

These are adaptations of UC-02, UC-04, UC-05 and UC-06 in the
[use-case guide](USE_CASES.md). UC-01 gifts and UC-03 naming remain useful product
prompts but are **not** supported by this narrow candidate set. A no-evidence answer
is the correct result when retrieved passages do not support the request.

## Expansion trigger

Add community-authored or community-reviewed material before testing community-specific motifs, terminology or traditional
practices. Record disagreements and the limits of each source in the review rubric;
do not broaden the claim merely because another region appears in a bibliography.
